The problem

Your agent got fast.
Your process is still a terminal tab and a good memory.

01

The work list lives somewhere else.

To see what is next, or to write a thought down, you leave the conversation. So you stop writing things down.

02

The agent decides when it is done.

It builds before you agreed on what to build, and commits before you looked. You find out in the diff.

03

The reasons die with the chat.

What you asked for, what was tried and why it was decided scrolls away. Next week, nobody knows. Including the agent.

Six journeys

A day with Airbender.

Not a feature list. What you actually do, from the first thought of the morning to the last commit, and what changes at each turn.

You are mid-conversation and notice something else that is broken. The field at the bottom of the tab is always there: type a title, press Enter, keep going. Tags in the title set the type, the priority and the category as you write.

02 · Hand off

Press play. Keep the wheel.

Every issue walks a fixed path, and at each step it waits for exactly one of you. The agent defines, plans, builds and reviews its own work. You approve the definition, the plan and the result. A small icon on every issue says whose move it is, so you never wonder who is waiting for whom.

And an issue only takes the steps its size needs. A typo fix reaches you once, at the end. A risky change stops at every turn.

Size

Large, risky or unclear: every step. You review the definition, then the plan, then the result.

Nothing has happened yet. The issue is one line you typed. Press play and the agent reads it, reads the code it touches, and sizes it.

03 · Decide

The decision sits where you are reading.

The agent finishes and says so. Right above the message box, one line tells you which issue this is, which step it is at and what is needed from you. The step's moves are buttons inside the box: approve, or send it back with the reason you just typed.

Or simply reply. If your reply asks for changes, the agent moves the issue back to the work. If it is only a question, the issue stays where it is.

04 · Multiply

Three issues. Three work trees. One clean main.

One agent on one issue is slow going. "Start in worktree" opens a new conversation for an issue, in a work tree and on a branch of its own, and leaves the conversation you were in alone. Start another. And another.

When you accept the work, the agent brings main into its branch, settles any conflict there, lands it and pushes. Then the conversation is archived, the work tree removed and the branch deleted. Nothing is left lying around.

05 · Go big

Take on an epic. Autopilot, with your hands on the reviews.

A migration with fourteen parts. Nest the parts under one issue, by dragging them. Give the whole epic one branch. Zoom in, and the tab shows only that epic.

Then switch on Autopilot. The epic's ready issues are started one after another, most urgent first, in the same conversation. Every review is still yours. If an issue gets stuck, it stops and tells you why, instead of digging. When the last one closes: "Close & merge".

06 · Remember

Chat is lost. The issue is not.

Three weeks later somebody asks why refunds go through a queue. The answer is not in a conversation you closed. It is on the issue: what you asked for in your own words, the plan, every decision with its reason, and what was tried and dropped.

The issues live inside your repository, kept by beads, and travel with it to your own git remote. Coming from GitHub Issues? Bring them in, comments and all, and send yours back the other way.

What it refuses to be

Opinionated.
On purpose.

  • An auto-closer.

    No issue closes without you: by your acceptance, or by your cancelling it. The agent commits only after you accepted and chose how.

  • A second tracker.

    Issues stay in beads, in your repository. The tab shows and changes them there. Change one in a terminal and the tab follows by itself.

  • A git tool.

    The plugin never commits. It asks your agent to, in the conversation, where you can see it happen.

  • A cloud service.

    There is no Airbender server. Your issues are in your repository and on your own git remote, nowhere else.

  • A cage.

    Too much ceremony for this repository? Switch it to Simple: no steps at all. Make current, release, close. Switch back and every issue is where it was.

  • A guessing machine.

    Open questions come first. Being started on an issue is not permission to assume the answers: the agent asks, and waits.

For the agents

They get a tool too.

bend is a small command agents use to read and change issues: list what is ready, write a plan, record a decision and its reason, hand work over to you. It works the same whatever tracker holds the issues, and it does not need the app at all.

$ bend ready
shop-2oy  P2  S  Remember the last shipping address
shop-b5o  P2  S  Empty cart page has no way back

$ bend decide shop-ty5.3 "Queue, not direct calls:
  a lost refund costs more than a slow one"

$ bend handover shop-ty5.3 \
    --done "Refunds go through the queue" \
    --checked "Retry, duplicate and timeout tests" \
    --not-checked "The provider's sandbox was down"
